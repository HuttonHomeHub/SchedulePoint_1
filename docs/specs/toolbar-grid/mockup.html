Plan toolbar grouping options

You said the toolbar looks awful, and named two causes: the grouping is messy, and there are stray bits. Below is today's toolbar redrawn at your monitor's width (1912 px, editing on), then three ways to fix the grouping. All three share the same clean-up of the stray bits. Nothing has been built yet.

What the review found

  • Every group is already meant to have a box. The five groups each sit on a rounded backing, but its edge is about 1.5 : 1 against the navy, so some groups look boxed and others don't.
  • Three different box styles compete. The faint group backing, the search field's own border, and a brighter outline drawn round Export and Share, which puts a box inside a box.
  • Most stray bits do a job, but don't look like it. The eye and wrench mark the two rows. The lone ▾ opens "Recent edits". The ± shows the baseline. The gear opens plan settings, but it sits next to Earned value as if it belonged to it.

The stray-bit clean-up, the same in every option

  • Remove the eye and wrench row marks. They are decoration, and the wrench is easy to mistake for an edit button.
  • Join the three Colour choices into one control, and show "Colour" at the same size as the buttons.
  • Give the ± a word: Baseline. You chose icon-only for it on 10 October, so this one needs your say-so.
  • Join undo, redo and Recent edits into one unit, and give Recent edits a clock icon instead of a bare ▾.
  • Show "Start milestone" with its diamond, without the repeated "Add:" (screen readers still hear "Add: Start milestone").
  • Move the settings gear to the end of the row, after Share, so it no longer reads as part of Earned value.
  • Drop the extra outline round Export and Share.
  • Keep the "…" on items that open a dialog. It is a documented rule, and it tells you a click won't act straight away.

Recommended

Dividers, switches and columns

My pick. Option B's quiet bar, with no group boxes, plus option C's recessed switches for anything where you choose one of several. Then one new idea: the two rows share columns. The first divider falls at the same point on both rows (the dashed amber guide is drawn here only to show it), and both right-hand groups end on the same edge, so the bar reads as one grid. Icons are a step quieter than their words, so the labels lead and the amber pen is the only loud thing on the bar.

no group boxes · recessed switches · shared column on both rowsrecommended

Why this one

  • Every shape on the bar is a control you operate, so it never looks decorated.
  • The aligned columns make the two rows read as one toolbar, not two lists.
  • The diagram stays the brightest thing on screen.

What it costs

  • Changes ADR-0185's group pill, so the toolbar ADR gets an amendment.
  • The shared column needs a measured width at each screen size, checked at 1024, 1646 and 1912.
  • Below 1024 the bar is one scrolling line, so the columns only apply above that.
Option A

Clear pills

Keep the five groups as they are, but make every group's box clearly visible: one box style, used the same way everywhere. Inside a group, a short divider separates the sections. This is the smallest change and follows the toolbar ADR's original intent.

one box tier · hairline dividers insideclosest to today

Good

  • Every group reads as a group at a glance.
  • Smallest change to code and tests.

Watch

  • Five outlined boxes are still a lot of boxes on a busy bar.
Option B

Dividers, no boxes

Take the group boxes away entirely. Groups are separated by a full-height divider and wider gaps. The only things with an outline are controls that are one unit: the Colour choice, the undo cluster, the split buttons and the search field.

no group containers · dividers between groupsquietest

Good

  • The calmest bar; an outline means "this is one control".
  • Works as well on the Surface as on the monitor.

Watch

  • Groups rely on gaps, so a squeezed row loses some of its shape.
  • Replaces ADR-0185's group pill, so it needs a short ADR amendment.
Option C

Shaded tiles

Each group sits on a slightly lighter tile with no outline at all. Choices that switch between options, like Colour, sit in a darker recessed track with the selected one raised, like a physical switch.

filled tiles · recessed switch tracksmost structured

Good

  • Groups are obvious without a single border line.
  • Selected states stand out more clearly than today.

Watch

  • The tile has to be bright enough to see on a dim screen, which means re-checking contrast.
  • The heaviest look of the three.
